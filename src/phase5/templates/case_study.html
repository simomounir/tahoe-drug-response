Why the result can be trusted

  • Rules first. Splits, metrics and the success criterion were written down before any model was trained; the $n_amendments later changes are logged with their reasons.
  • Baselines first. Five simple baselines were scored before the neural network, and ridge regression got the same inputs and a comparable tuning budget.
  • Uncertainty everywhere. Every split is drawn $n_repeats times; intervals come from $n_resamples bootstrap resamples within each repeat, and model comparisons are paired on the same conditions.

Data

Tahoe-100M single-cell profiles, aggregated per condition (cell line × drug × dose × plate) and compared with the DMSO control of the same line on the same plate. The evaluation set has $n_conditions conditions: $n_drugs drugs at $n_doses doses in $n_lines cell lines, each a log fold-change vector over $n_genes protein-coding genes. Differentially expressed genes come from Tahoe's DESeq2 table (median $median_de_genes per condition).

Inputs: drug structure (Morgan fingerprint, RDKit descriptors) and cell-line context from DepMap (expression principal components fitted on about 1,700 external lines, mutation flags). No input is derived from the measured response.

Evaluation design

Four splits hold out progressively more: random conditions, whole cell lines, whole drugs (with near-duplicate molecules kept together), and new drug plus new cell line at once. Validation data comes from the training side of each split; test data is scored once per model.

The headline metric is the Pearson correlation over each condition's differentially expressed genes; discrimination (can the prediction pick out its own condition among all test conditions) guards against models that predict one generic response. The pre-registered rule:
