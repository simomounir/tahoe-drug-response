# Tahoe-100M landscape review (Phase 3 background)

Research review only — no project code touched. Compiled 2026-09-25.

## Summary

- Tahoe-100M (Zhang et al., 2025, bioRxiv, CC0-1.0) is a 100M-cell atlas: 50 cancer cell lines × ~379 drugs × 3 doses (0.05/0.5/5.0 µM) across 14 plates, built on Vevo/Tahoe's Mosaic "cell village" platform. [from abstract/secondary]
- The dataset ships its own DESeq2-style pseudobulk DE table (`baseMean, log2FoldChange, lfcSE, stat, pvalue, padj`) computed **per plate**, per cell line, per drug, per concentration, treated vs. that plate's control cells — i.e. essentially the same logFC-vs-plate-matched-DMSO target this project computes independently. [verified: source read, HF datasets-server schema]
- At least three major follow-on efforts train/benchmark on Tahoe-100M: Arc Institute's STATE (Cell, 2026), Tahoe-x1 (bioRxiv, Oct 2025, foundation model to 3B params), and the Virtual Cell Challenge (Cell, 2025) whose held-out evaluation includes unseen cell-line contexts. [from abstract/secondary]
- The most load-bearing methodological warning for this project: Ahlmann-Eltze, Huber & Anders (Nature Methods, 2025) show deep-learning perturbation-effect predictors do not yet beat simple linear/mean baselines on comparable tasks — directly motivates Phase 4's baseline-vs-neural pre-registered design. [verified via search of primary abstract]
- A second, Tahoe-specific pitfall paper ("Score Distributions, Not Cells," arXiv 2607.04595) shows per-cell classifiers plateau near-chance on Tahoe/VCC perturbation discrimination while population-level (pseudobulk) profiles discriminate almost perfectly — a caution about which unit of aggregation a metric is computed on. [from abstract/secondary]

## 1. The Tahoe-100M paper

**Citation:** Zhang et al. (2025). "Tahoe-100M: A Giga-Scale Single-Cell Perturbation Atlas for Context-Dependent Gene Function and Cellular Modeling." *bioRxiv* 2025.02.20.639398 (posted Feb 24 2025; a v3 revision exists). https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.02.20.639398 [from abstract/secondary — bioRxiv itself returned HTTP 429 to repeated fetch attempts, so this is built from search-engine summaries and the HF dataset card/README, not a direct read of the manuscript text]

Design, as reported across secondary sources and cross-checked against the HF dataset schema:
- ~100M single-cell transcriptomic profiles (100.6M post-QC), 50 cancer cell lines pooled together per plate ("cell village"/Mosaic platform to reduce batch effects), 379 distinct drugs (of ~1,100+ nominally profiled/available across the broader Tahoe program), at 3 doses each (0.05, 0.5, 5.0 µM), across 14 96-well plates (~93–95 drugs + DMSO controls per plate). [from abstract/secondary]
- 69% of compounds are human-approved agents; 180 of 379 drugs map to 25 broad MOA categories, median 5 drugs/MOA. [from abstract/secondary]
- QC filters described for Tahoe/sci-Plex-style pseudobulk pipelines in a downstream paper (not confirmed as the original paper's exact wording): ngenes ≥ 250, ncounts ≥ 700, mitochondrial fraction ≤ 0.2, MALAT1 expression filter log(1+10000·f_MALAT1) ≥ 3.5. [from abstract/secondary — attributed to a re-processing paper, flagged here as uncertain provenance]
- I could not confirm, from what I was able to read, that the original paper itself reports a predictive benchmark/baseline model for drug-response transcriptomics (its stated contribution is the atlas plus biological analyses of context-dependent gene function). Do not assume it contains a baseline number to compare against without a direct read of the manuscript.

## 4. Known pitfalls in drug-perturbation evaluation

- **Deep learning vs. simple baselines:** Ahlmann-Eltze, Huber & Anders (Nature Methods 2025) is the single most important citation here — across 5 foundation models + 2 deep models, none beat linear/mean-of-training-data baselines on unseen-gene / unseen-combination perturbation prediction. Directly supports pre-registering baselines (mean-per-drug, mean-per-cell-line, nearest-neighbor-by-chemistry) as first-class comparators, not throwaway ablations. [from abstract/secondary, corroborated by multiple independent sources]
- **Statistical vs. practical baseline dominance in a live challenge:** Virtual Cell Challenge 2025 wrap-up reports winning teams found pure deep-learning approaches did not consistently beat statistical baselines on DES/MAE, and had to blend in classical DEG-frequency/mean-expression features to win. [from abstract/secondary]
- **Aggregation-level pitfalls (Tahoe-specific):** "Score Distributions, Not Cells" shows per-cell classification metrics on Tahoe/VCC data can look near-chance (macro-F1 0.2–0.3) purely from cell-to-cell overlap/noise, while the *same* models achieve near-perfect discrimination once scores are aggregated to the population/pseudobulk level (CDS ≈ 0.98–1.0). Implication: metric choice must match the biological unit of interest — for a pseudobulk logFC-prediction task like ours, per-gene/per-condition (pseudobulk) correlation is the right unit, not per-cell agreement. [from abstract/secondary]
- **Generic/"mean-baseline" and all-gene Pearson pitfalls (general knowledge, not newly sourced this session):** Pearson correlation computed over all ~20k genes is dominated by the many near-zero, non-DE genes, inflating apparent accuracy for any model (including a mean/no-op baseline) that just predicts "no change" almost everywhere; the field's emerging convention (consistent with our own DE-gene-restricted Pearson + top-k overlap + discrimination-score plan) is to restrict correlation/overlap metrics to the DE gene set and/or use rank/discrimination-based scores (PDS/CDS-style) that are less dominated by the bulk of unperturbed genes. This is a general, widely-discussed point in the perturbation-modelling literature (e.g., the PDS metric originates with Wu et al. 2024, cited by both STATE and the CDS paper above); I did not independently re-verify the Wu et al. 2024 primary source in this session, so flag it as [from abstract/secondary].
- **Unseen-drug generalization:** Recommended practice, consistent with STATE/VCC and general search results, is to hold out drug identity entirely (all doses, all cell lines for that drug removed from train/val) rather than only holding out dose or replicate — this matches our `unseen_drug` split design.

## 5. What would make this project's contribution distinct

Given the small scale (8 cell lines × 92 drugs × 3 doses, laptop-scale, single project brief), honest differentiation angles:

1. **Pre-registered, baseline-first evaluation discipline at small scale.** Nearly every published Tahoe-adjacent benchmark (STATE, Tahoe-x1, VCC) targets giga-scale foundation models; none of the sources found here describe a small, fully pre-registered baseline-vs-neural comparison with fixed splits (random/unseen_drug/unseen_cell_line/both_unseen) and bootstrap CIs at this scale. Explicitly replicating the Ahlmann-Eltze-style "does the neural model actually beat the linear/mean baseline" question, but on a hand-picked, tractable slice with full transparency, is a legitimate and currently under-represented contribution — especially as a portfolio piece demonstrating rigor rather than scale.
2. **Cross-checking a from-scratch pseudobulk/DE pipeline against the dataset's official DE table.** No source found here describes anyone using the official `pseudobulk_differential_expression` table as a validation oracle for an independently computed logFC pipeline. Since the official table appears to be per-plate DESeq2 (inferred from column names) matching our own plate-matched-DMSO design, this is a genuinely useful, novel-to-this-project QA step (see implications below) rather than a novel scientific claim.
3. **Honest scope framing.** At 8 lines × 92 drugs, this project cannot claim generalization comparable to STATE/Tahoe-x1's held-out-cell-line claims (trained on 50–70 lines). The defensible contribution is methodological: a clean, reproducible, small-scale demonstration of evaluation hygiene (DE-gene-restricted metrics, discrimination scores, unseen-drug/cell-line splits, bootstrap CIs) rather than a state-of-the-art accuracy claim — and being explicit about that in the writeup is itself part of the contribution given how much of the literature (per point 4 above) is currently about baselines being under-beaten.

## Implications for phase 3 and the evaluation spec

- **Use the official `pseudobulk_differential_expression` table as a cross-check/QA oracle, not as the primary DE-gene source for training**, at least initially: its schema (`gene_name, baseMean, log2FoldChange, lfcSE, stat, pvalue, padj`, keyed by `plate, drug, concentration, Cell_Name_Vevo`) is per-plate DESeq2 output vs. matched controls — pull a handful of (drug, cell line, dose) rows for cell lines/drugs already in our slice and compare `log2FoldChange`/`padj` against our own from-scratch pseudobulk logFC computation as a correctness check before trusting our pipeline for Phase 4 splits. Confirm independently (by checking a `drug` value) whether "control" in that table specifically means DMSO before treating it as equivalent to our own DMSO-matched baseline.
- **Adopt the official table's DE-gene definition (`padj`-thresholded) as one candidate definition of "DE genes" for the DE-gene-restricted Pearson/top-k metrics**, alongside/compared against whatever threshold our own pipeline uses, so Phase 4 metrics are defensible against an external, citable reference rather than only self-consistent.
- **Our 8-line/92-drug/3-plate slice is not directly comparable to any published headline number** (STATE, Tahoe-x1, VCC results are computed on the full 50-line/379-drug atlas or other datasets entirely); do not present our metrics as "beating" or "comparable to" those papers' numbers in the writeup — instead position against the mean/linear baselines computed in-house, per Ahlmann-Eltze et al.'s precedent.
- **Metric design is already aligned with the field's stated pitfalls**: DE-gene-restricted Pearson (avoids all-gene Pearson dominated by non-DE genes), top-k overlap, and a discrimination metric (population-level, not per-cell) all match recommendations/cautions found in the STATE PDS approach and the "Score Distributions, Not Cells" paper. Make sure any discrimination metric is computed at the pseudobulk/profile level, not per-cell, per that paper's explicit warning.
- **Pre-register the mean-per-drug and mean-per-cell-line baselines as first-class comparators** in the Phase 4 spec (not just a neural model), citing Ahlmann-Eltze et al. (2025) and the VCC 2025 wrap-up as direct precedent for why this comparison is scientifically expected, not optional.
- Treat the exact QC-filter numbers (ngenes≥250 etc.) and the "DESeq2 was used" claim as **inferred/secondary, not confirmed primary-source facts** — bioRxiv blocked repeated direct fetches (HTTP 429) in this session, so the original manuscript's methods section was not read verbatim. If methodological precision matters later (e.g., matching their exact QC before comparing our logFC numbers), fetch https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.02.20.639398 directly in a fresh session/IP.
